TipHow to read these notes

Each lecture is a standalone page written to be read straight through. The recording stays one click away: the chapter links its video under the title, and every section carries a timestamp that opens the lecture at the moment the idea is introduced, so you can watch the derivation being built when the prose is not enough. Sections, equations and figures are numbered and individually linkable.

What these notes are

The official CS229 notes are the formal account of the course, and a good one. What they cannot carry is the lecture itself: the order in which Andrew Ng motivates an idea before he formalises it, the picture he draws to make an update rule obvious, the question from the room that exposes the step everyone was about to skip.

These notes are written from the Autumn 2018 recordings rather than from the official notes, and they are meant to sit beside both. Each page aims to be:

  • Faithful to the lecture — it follows what was taught and why, and links each section to the moment in the video where it happens.
  • Complete in its mathematics — the derivations done at the board are written out in full, with every symbol defined.
  • A bridge, not a copy — where the official notes go further, the chapter says which section to read next rather than reproducing it.

The whole book is also a single PDF. For an AI assistant, llms.txt maps every chapter to a Markdown copy with the maths in TeX, and llms-full.txt holds the book in one file. The same treatment of Stanford’s deep learning for computer vision course is in the companion CS231n 2025 Lecture Notes.

Roadmap

  1. Supervised learning — linear and logistic regression, generalized linear models, generative learning, support vector machines and kernels
  2. Generalization — bias and variance, model selection, learning theory
  3. Trees and neural networks — decision trees and ensembles, neural networks and backpropagation
  4. Applying machine learning — debugging learning algorithms and error analysis
  5. Unsupervised learning — k-means, EM, factor analysis, PCA and ICA
  6. Reinforcement learning — MDPs, value and policy iteration, continuous states, LQR
